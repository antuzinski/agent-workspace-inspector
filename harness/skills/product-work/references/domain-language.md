# Clarify domain language

First check the project's existing definitions. Create or extend a project glossary only when recurring ambiguity changes a decision, calculation or product behavior. Record the term, its meaning in this project, and an example or exclusion that resolves the ambiguity.

For example: “customer” means a paying organization, while “user” means an individual account. A company with ten accounts counts as one customer and ten users; mixing them changes retention and revenue per customer.

When the meaning is disputed, preserve the competing definitions and their consequences until evidence or the owner resolves them. Do not silently choose a definition to make a calculation work.

Save a short decision record only when the choice is costly to reverse, its reason would be unclear later, and meaningful alternatives existed. Record the choice, reason, rejected alternative, tradeoff and condition for reconsideration. Follow the project's documentation conventions; do not create global glossary or decision files for every task.
