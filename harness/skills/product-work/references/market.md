# Market sizing and alternatives

Define the market in terms of a specific customer, need or job, geography, and time period. Include direct, indirect, and status-quo alternatives when they affect the real choice. State exclusions and note when a boundary is uncertain.

For TAM, SAM, and SOM, keep the underlying unit, value basis, and period comparable. For example, do not express TAM/SAM as gross transaction value and SOM as platform revenue. If the business model means a conversion is needed, show the conversion explicitly and report the comparable market measure separately. Define TAM as the full relevant market, SAM as the part the offering can serve under stated constraints, and SOM as the realistically obtainable share over a stated horizon; explain the constraint behind each narrowing.

Prefer a transparent bottom-up estimate when credible counts and value per unit are available; use top-down figures as a cross-check, not a substitute for defining the market. One method can be enough. Show source, date, units, arithmetic, assumptions, and confidence; identify which assumptions drive the range. If inputs are unavailable, give a formula or bounded scenario and say what data would resolve it. Never manufacture precision. Explain material disagreement after aligning definitions, periods, and units.

Competitive comparisons should focus on the alternatives relevant to the decision. Distinguish sourced facts from interpretation, and include strengths that could make an alternative preferable. A map or exhaustive competitor list is optional, only if it clarifies the question.
