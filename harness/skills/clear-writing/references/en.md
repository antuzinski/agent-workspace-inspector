# English editing

- Preserve meaning, confidence, and causal relationships. Do not turn correlation into causation or remove a qualification just to smooth the prose.
- Prefer familiar words and name the actor: who checks, decides, or changes something.
- Briefly explain a necessary technical term when it first appears. Do not replace an understandable idea with a chain of labels or acronyms.
- Add one concrete example for an abstract idea when it helps the reader apply it.
- Remove canned openings, filler transitions, and sentences that merely announce the explanation to come. Preserve the author's voice and purposeful repetition.
- Do not make prose so telegraphic that it loses the links between cause, action, and consequence.
